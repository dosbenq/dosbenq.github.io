# The Long Way Round: voiceover script

About 85 seconds. Each block lines up with a scene in the film. The words are a guide, not a script to read word for word: say each block the way you'd tell a friend, and change anything that doesn't sound like you. That's what will keep it from sounding like AI.

## How to record (10 minutes, just a phone)

1. Find the quietest small room you have. A closet full of clothes is perfect; bare kitchens echo.
2. Use your phone's voice memo app. Hold the phone about a hand-span (20 cm) from your mouth, slightly to the side.
3. Record each block as its own take, or all in one file with a 2-second pause between blocks. Do two or three takes of anything you stumble on.
4. Smile while you talk. It really does come through.
5. Send me the file(s). I'll clean up the noise, level the voice, lower the music under it, and adjust the scene timing to match your pace.

## Script

**1. Title (0:00–0:06)**
> Hey, I'm Aditya. Let me tell you how I got here. It's been the long way round.

**2. Delhi (0:06–0:16)**
> I was always the analytical kid. Math, science, building fun stuff. So: engineering. But I also loved investing, and how money moves the world. So I added economics.

**3. Bengaluru (0:16–0:28)**
> Then, five years at HARMAN in Bengaluru, building navigation for cars and trucks. One thing I'm proud of: when a truck's camera can't see a speed sign, our map data still knows it.

**4. The doubt (0:28–0:36)**
> But I kept having business doubts. As an engineer, you get tasks, not a vote. And a lot of effort went into things that just didn't make business sense.

**5. Recalculating (0:36–0:44)**
> I wanted to help decide *what* gets built, not just *how*. So I recalculated.

**6. The flight (0:44–0:51)**
> In 2025 I moved to the US. It's where AI is being built, and I wanted to be there.

**7. Duke (0:51–1:05)**
> At Duke, engineering management gave me the business side: strategy, finance, marketing. Design Thinking taught me how to really interview customers. After fifteen-plus interviews, the lesson was simple: people weren't missing information. They were missing trust.

**8. PointsMax (1:05–1:14)**
> And on the side, I'm a little obsessed with credit card points. I was optimizing everything by hand, couldn't find a tool that did it, so I built one. PointsMax.

**9. Destination (1:14–1:25)**
> Next stop? A team that moves fast, has fun, and makes a real dent. Maybe yours.
