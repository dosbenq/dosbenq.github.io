    Aug 2016 – May 2020 · New Delhi

    Two lenses from day one.

    B.Tech in Electronics & Communication Engineering at IIIT Delhi, with a minor in Economics. Signal processing on one side of the timetable; game theory, econometrics and microeconomics on the other.

    That pairing still shapes how I work: how does the system behave, and why would anyone choose it?

    Oct 2020 – Mar 2023 · Bengaluru · Associate Software Engineer

    Navigation and ADAS for trucks and cars on three continents.

    Location-Based Services at HARMAN (a Samsung company): the software that knows the road, the speed limit and what’s around the next bend.

    i

    The camera goes blind. The map doesn’t.

    For MAN trucks across the EU, I delivered Traffic Sign Information: sign data from the map, fed into HARMAN’s platform and exposed via API, so the truck still knows the limit when its camera can’t see. Try it.

    100% of signs known · camera sees them all

    The watchdog that kept biting.

    Field crashes were slow to trace, so I wrote a Python tool that parses ADAS logs automatically. It led us to a critical watchdog-timer bug. Fixing it cut crash frequency by 50%.

    $500K secured. Then: road closed.

    I led a six-week proof of concept for an Intelligent Speed Assistance protocol suite, tied to the EU ISA mandate. My feasibility and build-vs-partner analysis won $500K in development funding. Then OEM timelines shifted and the program was shelved. Good analysis doesn’t guarantee the road stays open.

    Mar 2023 – Jul 2025 · Bengaluru · Product Engineer

    Less code, more calls about what ships.

    I owned cross-functional delivery for an augmented-reality navigation feature for global OEM customers, aligning teams in the US, EU and India on requirements, API dependencies and release scope.

    38 Sprint velocity, up from 28. I built the Python KPI dashboards that made it visible.
    95% Sprint commitments delivered across 24 sprints
    −4 mo Release cycle, after I moved FTP releases to Jenkins CI/CD
    5 × 3 Audi ADAS change requests sequenced across 3 markets

    On Audi’s global ADAS program, the hard part was the tradeoff: keep legacy OEM commitments, or invest in E-Horizon, a next-generation Android navigation architecture. I sequenced the work against regulatory deadlines and engineering capacity so each release stayed on schedule.

    E-Horizon uses map data to let a car see past the next curve. The “Then” list in the bar at the top of this page does the same thing for the drive.

    Jan 2026 – now · Solo founder

    PointsMax: navigation for your points.
